
Flight is the movement of an aircraft through the air. Engineers describe flight using forces, motion, and energy.

Lift acts approximately perpendicular to the relative airflow. Wing shape and angle of attack influence lift.

Drag opposes motion through air. The amount of drag depends on factors such as speed and aircraft shape.

Thrust is produced by propulsion systems. A propeller accelerates air and produces a reaction force.

Gravity pulls an aircraft toward Earth. Aircraft must manage the effects of weight throughout a flight.

Pitch describes rotation about the aircraft lateral axis. Pitch changes the orientation of the nose.

Roll describes rotation about the longitudinal axis. Roll changes the aircraft bank angle.

Yaw describes rotation about the vertical axis. Yaw changes the direction the nose points.

An inertial measurement unit can measure angular motion and acceleration. These readings can drift over time.

A flight controller uses sensor measurements to estimate vehicle state. Its control algorithms respond to disturbances.

A ground control station displays flight information to an operator. It may support mission planning and log inspection.

Telemetry is information transmitted from an aircraft to another system. It may include position and battery measurements.
